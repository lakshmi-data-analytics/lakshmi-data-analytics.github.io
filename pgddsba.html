PGDDSBA Learning Journey

A record of my learning journey through the Post Graduate Diploma in Data Science and Business Analytics, focusing on the concepts I studied, how my understanding developed, and the practical work through which I applied them.

1. Essentials of MS Excel

I developed practical proficiency in Microsoft Excel for data preparation, analysis, visualization and decision-making. My learning progressed from working with structured datasets to using advanced formulas, lookup techniques, analytical tools, Solver and financial data features.

Building an analytical workflow

I worked with Excel for organizing, cleaning and manipulating datasets, using sorting, filtering, data validation and conditional formatting. I then moved into formula-based analysis using functions such as IFERROR, INDEX, MATCH, XLOOKUP, VLOOKUP, HLOOKUP and SUMIFS.

I also learned to use PivotTables, the Data Analysis ToolPak, Data Tables and Solver for analytical and decision-support problems.

Working with financial data

As part of the bridge-course Excel learning, I worked with Excel's Stocks data type and the STOCKHISTORY function. This allowed me to convert stock names into structured stock data and retrieve historical market information for analysis.

What this developed

The ability to use Excel as an analytical tool for preparing data, performing advanced lookups and calculations, analysing financial data, solving decision problems and communicating findings through tables and visualizations.

2. Mathematical Foundations — Bridge Course

This bridge course provided the mathematical foundation required for the analytical subjects that followed. I worked with number systems, set theory and set operations, De Morgan's laws, matrices and matrix operations, systems of linear equations, functions and equations.

I also gained introductory experience using Excel Solver to solve problems involving equations and constraints.

This course served as a foundation rather than a comprehensive mathematics-for-data-science program. My deeper study of mathematics for data science, including linear algebra and calculus, is being developed separately through independent learning.

3. Foundations of Business Analytics

This course built my foundation in using statistical methods to understand and interpret business data. I learned how to organize and summarize data, describe distributions, work with probability, and use sampling and estimation to draw conclusions from data.

Understanding data statistically

I worked with frequency distributions and different forms of visualization, along with measures of central tendency and dispersion. The course also introduced probability concepts, conditional probability, Bayes' theorem and probability distributions including Binomial, Poisson and Normal distributions.

Sampling concepts, sampling distributions, the Central Limit Theorem, standard error and point and interval estimation helped me understand how information from a sample can be used to reason about a larger population.

Extending the learning through Coursera

As part of the coursework, I also completed two complementary Coursera courses from Rice University. Business Applications of Hypothesis Testing and Confidence Interval Estimation — Rice University (Coursera) strengthened my understanding of statistical inference and hypothesis testing, while Introduction to Data Analysis Using Excel — Rice University (Coursera) reinforced the practical use of Excel for data analysis.

These courses complemented the classroom learning by connecting statistical concepts with practical analytical workflows.

Practical application

I worked through multiple Excel-based examples to apply the statistical concepts covered in the course. For a major assignment, I was provided with a consumer preference and satisfaction dataset containing approximately 2,500 observations and 37 columns.

I performed descriptive analysis using summary statistics, PivotTables and charts and prepared a report presenting insights from the data for a business decision-making context.

What this developed

The ability to use descriptive statistics, probability, sampling and estimation to explore business data and communicate evidence-based insights using Excel.

4. Fundamentals of Data Science & Decision Making

This course moved from foundational statistics toward more advanced statistical analysis for business decision-making. I learned how statistical methods can be used to formulate questions, test claims, investigate differences between groups and support decisions using evidence from data.

From hypotheses to statistical decisions

A major part of the coursework focused on hypothesis testing. I followed a structured five-step approach: stating the null and alternative hypotheses, selecting the level of significance, determining the critical value, calculating the test statistic and p-value, and finally making and interpreting the statistical decision.

Through textbook-based business problems, I worked with one-tailed and two-tailed tests, Z-tests, t-tests, F-tests and tests involving two samples.

Comparing groups and understanding variation

The course expanded statistical reasoning into experimental design and Analysis of Variance. I learned one-way and two-way ANOVA, randomized block designs and factorial approaches, along with chi-square methods for categorical data.

I also received an introduction to Operations Research, including the formulation of Linear Programming problems and their graphical solution.

Practical learning

The coursework involved multiple Excel exercises based on business problems from textbooks. I used Microsoft Excel and JMP to apply the statistical methods and interpret their results.

Case-study application

As part of the course, I also participated in a case-study presentation as part of a seven-member team. Each team was assigned a separate business case. Our case required us to approach a business problem through statistical analysis and forecasting.

The work involved translating a business problem into testable claims, selecting appropriate statistical tests, forecasting demand, interpreting the resulting evidence and connecting the analytical findings back to the underlying business decision.

Without reproducing the case material itself, the analytical techniques applied included pooled-variance and two-sample t-tests, ANOVA, chi-square analysis, simple moving averages and simple exponential smoothing.

What this developed

The ability to approach a business problem statistically: formulate the question, select an appropriate analytical method, perform the analysis, evaluate the evidence and connect the resulting insights to a business decision.

5. Introduction to R

This course introduced me to R as a programming environment for data analysis and statistical computing. I developed the ability to work with data programmatically, explore datasets, perform statistical analysis and communicate analytical findings.

Building the R foundation

I learned the fundamentals of R, including vectors and matrices, loading packages, data entry and exporting data, grouping, conditional execution, loops and functions.

I then moved into exploratory data analysis, summary statistics, probability and distributions, followed by inferential statistical methods such as one-sample and two-sample t-tests, Wilcoxon tests, paired t-tests, one-way ANOVA and the Kruskal-Wallis test.

Working with real datasets

A significant part of the coursework involved working directly with data. I learned how to import and export CSV and Excel files, clean and transform data, explore variables and create visualizations.

I also used built-in datasets such as Iris, Cars and Palmer Penguins to apply analytical concepts and practice working with different types of data.

Extending the learning through Coursera

As part of the coursework, I also completed Data Analysis with R Programming — Google (Coursera). This complemented the classroom learning by strengthening my understanding of R for data analysis and introducing me to the tidyverse ecosystem and Markdown-based analytical workflows.

The combination of the classroom R coursework and the Coursera learning helped me move beyond individual commands toward a more structured approach to data preparation, analysis, visualization and communicating results.

Statistical analysis

I used R to perform correlation and simple linear regression, interpret the results and examine residuals to assess regression assumptions.

Team mini-project

The practical mini-project was completed as a three-member team. We collected a dataset from Kaggle and used the Mental Health Lifestyle dataset to perform exploratory data analysis, inferential statistics and regression analysis in RStudio.

We interpreted the results and presented our findings to the class through a PowerPoint presentation.

What this developed

The ability to use R as an analytical environment, from importing and preparing datasets to exploratory analysis, statistical testing, regression, visualization and communicating analytical findings.

6. Data Visualization & Power BI

This course introduced me to the role of data visualization in analytical decision-making and helped me understand that effective visualization is not simply about creating charts, but about choosing appropriate visual representations and communicating insights clearly.

Learning through practical sessions

I applied visualization principles through multiple hands-on Power BI classroom exercises using datasets provided as part of the coursework. These practical sessions helped me develop experience in transforming datasets into reports, selecting appropriate visualizations, designing interactive dashboards and communicating analytical findings.

Extending the learning through data modeling

Alongside the classroom work, the Data Modeling in Power BI Coursera course strengthened my understanding of what happens behind the visual layer of a report.

I learned about data modeling, relationships, schemas and DAX measures, which helped me understand how a well-structured data model supports more meaningful and flexible analysis.

Practical application

I applied these skills to a banking performance analysis team project, using financial and stock-market data to explore the performance of leading private-sector banks.

I am currently redeveloping and extending this analysis independently, including an additional financial year. Once the redevelopment is finalized, the completed project will be added to the portfolio as a standalone project with its own GitHub repository.

What this developed

The ability to move from raw datasets to structured analytical models and interactive dashboards, combining data modeling, DAX measures and visualization to communicate business insights effectively.

7. Data Mining for Business Intelligence

This was my introduction to Data Mining for Business Intelligence and established an important foundation for the Artificial Intelligence & Machine Learning course that followed.

The course focused on understanding how raw data can be prepared, transformed and reduced before analytical or mining techniques are applied.

Preparing data for analysis

I learned the different stages of data preprocessing, including data cleaning and transformation, normalization, handling categorical and text information, and preparing datasets for subsequent analysis.

The course also introduced data reduction techniques, including dimensionality reduction and numerosity reduction using Rough Set Theory. Principal Component Analysis was explored from its mathematical foundation.

I was also introduced to techniques for handling class imbalance, including oversampling, undersampling and SMOTE, along with approaches to outlier detection.

Discovering patterns and working with text

I learned association-rule mining and concepts such as support, confidence and lift to understand relationships within transactional data.

The course also introduced text-mining concepts including the Vector Space Model, Bag of Words, TF-IDF and word embeddings.

Through these topics, I began to see how data mining can move beyond simply predicting an outcome and instead focus on discovering useful patterns and relationships hidden within data.

Business intelligence foundations

The course also provided a theoretical introduction to concepts related to data warehousing, OLAP and business-intelligence architecture, helping me understand how data storage, preparation, mining and interpretation fit together within a broader analytical workflow.

Practical application

As a team project, I worked on Amazon Reviews Sentiment Analysis using a Multinomial Naive Bayes classifier. We worked through the classification problem using the mathematical formulation taught in class, tested the classification using Python, evaluated the results using a confusion matrix and presented the findings.

What this developed

An understanding of the data-mining journey—from preparing and reducing raw data to discovering patterns, working with text, applying classification and understanding how data mining fits within a broader business-intelligence workflow.

8. Artificial Intelligence & Machine Learning

This course introduced me to the foundations of Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning, with a strong emphasis on understanding how different learning algorithms work and the mathematical intuition behind them.

I learned the distinction between supervised and unsupervised learning, along with concepts such as model selection, training and evaluation. Much of the coursework focused on understanding the underlying logic, mathematical formulation and architecture of different models rather than treating algorithms as black boxes.

I studied supervised-learning approaches including linear regression, logistic regression, Decision Trees, Random Forests, K-Nearest Neighbours, Support Vector Machines and Naive Bayes.

The course also introduced unsupervised learning through approaches such as K-Means, K-Modes and DBSCAN clustering, along with Principal Component Analysis for dimensionality reduction.

Association-rule mining was also encountered as part of the broader data-mining concepts, providing another perspective on discovering patterns within data.

Practical application

The primary hands-on machine-learning implementation was a three-member group project focused on business-failure prediction using Support Vector Machines.

Working with Python, we followed the modelling process from data preparation through model development and evaluation. This connected the mathematical and conceptual understanding of SVM from the classroom with an actual predictive classification problem.

What this developed

A conceptual and mathematical understanding of major machine-learning approaches, together with practical experience applying SVM to a predictive classification problem using Python.
